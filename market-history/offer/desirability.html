Check an address

Enter a house number and street in the selected town. Two independent geocoders are asked; it is placed only if they agree and the result lands inside the town.

Units

Analytical hexagons generated for this map — about 140 m across in Springfield and Colonia, about 225 m in Wayne because the township is roughly five times larger. They are not neighborhoods and have no official status.

Factors (each 0–100, 100 is always the good end)

Housing value: nearby sold prices, weighted by 1/distance² out to 2 km and averaged in logs, shrunk toward the town median where few sales are close. Single-family only — condo buildings stack dozens of sales on one coordinate and would swamp every cell around them. Sales cover three years and are time-adjusted to the latest month on each town's own median trend, so the map measures where rather than when. Not size-adjusted.

Major-road exposure: distance to motorway/trunk roads (influence to 450 m) and primary/secondary arterials (to 200/120 m). Higher = quieter.

Non-residential adjacency: distance to commercial, retail or industrial land, fading out by 250 m.

Flood: share of each cell inside FEMA's 1% or 0.2% annual-chance zones, sampled at 7 points.

Amenities: distance to the nearest public park, school, and everyday grocery stop (Wawa, Trader Joe's, Seabra).

The headline number is a rank

The five factors are absolute scores. The composite is then converted to a percentile within the town: 90 means "better than 90% of the liveable cells here," not 90 out of 100. A yellow cell in Wayne and a yellow cell in Colonia both mean "middling for here" — use the market snapshot for cross-town price levels.

Government-assisted housing

HUD's three registers of subsidised property — tax-credit (LIHTC), public housing, and project-based Section 8 — deduplicated, sized by unit count. It is a layer you can switch on, never a scoring factor: the map will not rank a neighbourhood up or down for it. What the composite already measures, and what actually moves prices, is density, traffic and what sold nearby.

An empty map does not mean there is none. New Jersey's Mount Laurel obligation is mostly met through inclusionary zoning — a developer builds 200 market-rate apartments and deed-restricts 30. Those units sit in ordinary buildings with no federal subsidy and appear in no HUD register. Only standalone assisted properties show up here.

"For sale" means for sale

A listing feed's "active" is not what you can buy — pending and contingent listings already have an accepted offer. Those are hidden here by default and greyed when you switch them on, the same rule market.html uses. Nothing is mixed in silently.

Not scored

Cells sitting on shopping centres, factories, golf courses, cemeteries, school grounds or large parks are drawn gray and left out of the rankings — nobody lives there.

School quality is deliberately absent: our ratings are district deciles per zip, identical for every house in a town, so they cannot say which street to buy on. Crime and attendance zones are out for the same reason.
