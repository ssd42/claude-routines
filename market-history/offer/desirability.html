Units

Analytical hexagons generated for this map (about 140 m across in Springfield and Colonia, about 225 m in Wayne because the township is roughly five times larger). They are not neighborhoods and have no official status.

Factors (each 0–100)

Housing value: inverse-distance-weighted log sale price of recent single-family sales, shrunk toward the town median where few sales are nearby. Condos, likely townhouse-community sales and land are excluded. Prices are not size-adjusted.

Major-road exposure: distance to motorway/trunk roads (influence to 450 m) and primary/secondary arterials (to 200/120 m). Higher = quieter.

Non-residential adjacency: distance to OSM commercial, retail or industrial land use, fading out by 250 m.

Flood: share of each cell in FEMA's 1% (Special Flood Hazard Area) or 0.2% zones, sampled at 7 points. If FEMA does not respond, distance to OSM streams is used and labeled as a proxy.

Amenities: straight-line distance to the nearest public park/field and nearest school.

Left out on purpose

Resident sentiment (not enough location-specific evidence; listing copy is marketing), crime (only town-level data), and school attendance zones (not reliably published for all three towns). Weight 0 for each.

Why towns can't be compared by color

Each town is ranked against itself. A yellow cell in Wayne and a yellow cell in Colonia are both "middle for their town"; use the market snapshot for cross-town price levels.
