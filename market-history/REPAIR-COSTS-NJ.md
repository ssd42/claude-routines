# What things cost — North/Central NJ

A reference for turning *"needs work"* into a number. When a house is priced low, or the
listing says "as-is," this is what fixing it actually costs around here.

**Where this applies:** Union, Essex, Morris, Somerset, Middlesex — the NYC commuter belt.
Prices are 2025–2026.

---

## Before you use any number in here

**1. NJ costs more, and here's the honest size of it.** Materials cost the same here as
anywhere (index ~100). **Labor runs 28–35% above the national average.** Blended, this work
runs **~13% above national**.

| Area | Labor index | Blended |
|---|---|---|
| Newark (Essex) | 134.2 | 114.3 |
| Elizabeth (Union) | 135.2 | 114.6 |
| Summit (Union) | 132.3 | 113.3 |
| Dover (Morris) | 135.3 | 114.5 |
| **New Brunswick (Middlesex)** | **127.8** | **111.5** |

*Source: [RSMeans City Cost Index](https://www.rsmeans.com/media/wysiwyg/quarterly_updates/2021-CCI-LocationFactors-V2.pdf), plumbing/HVAC divisions. National = 100.
This is the best-sourced number in the document. Caveat: the table is the 2021 edition, so
read it as relative position between towns, not 2026 dollars.*

Practical rule: **take a national figure, add ~13%.** Labor-heavy jobs skew higher; a
straight equipment swap skews lower. **Middlesex is measurably cheaper** than the
Essex/Union/Morris core — about 3 points, for being outside the immediate NYC labor shed.

**2. Most "average cost" websites are advertising.** Angi, HomeAdvisor, HomeGuide, Fixr and
Thumbtack are lead-generation businesses — their numbers come from advertisers, not surveys.
They're labeled **[lead-gen]** wherever used. The genuinely good sources are:
- **Cost vs. Value Report** (Zonda/Remodeling, 38th annual) — the industry-standard survey,
  broken out by metro. The **New York metro** column covers our towns.
- **NJDEP / EPA / NJ DCA** — for anything regulatory.
- **Published NJ contractor price lists** — marketing, but closer to the ground than national estimators.

**3. Where sources disagree by 2x or more, both numbers are shown.** That spread is usually
real — it means they're describing different jobs — so it isn't averaged away.

**4. These are planning ranges, not quotes.** The most accurate number you will ever have is
**a real quote on a real house.** Log them as they come in; three of your own quotes beat
every table below.

**5. Two things changed for 2026 — ignore any guide that says otherwise:**
- The **federal 25C efficiency credit** (windows, HVAC, insulation) **expired 12/31/2025.**
- The **federal 30% solar credit (25D) expired 12/31/2025.**
Any cost guide netting these out is a year stale.

## Central air conditioning

**The single most important question: does the house already have ductwork?** It's the
difference between a $7,500 job and a $25,000 one, and it's the main reason a charming
1920s house is cheaper than it looks.

### If it has forced-air heat (ducts already there)

| | Cost |
|---|---|
| Low | $4,500 |
| **Typical** | **$6,000 – $9,500** |
| High | $13,000 – $16,000 |

By house size: 1,000–1,500 sf → $4,500–7,500 · 1,500–2,000 sf → $5,500–9,500 · 2,000–2,500 sf
→ $6,500–11,500 · 2,500–3,000 sf → $7,500–13,500 · 3,000+ sf → $9,000–16,000+

**Watch for:** ducts sized for heating are often **undersized for cooling** — the returns are
the usual failure. Duct modification adds **$1,500–$4,500**, and it's reportedly needed in
40–70% of pre-1990 homes. If the furnace is 15+ years old you're realistically buying both;
done in the same visit the furnace adds only **$1,200–$2,500** rather than a second full job.

> **Rule of thumb:** listing says *"forced air, no central air"* → **budget $7,500.** A quote
> under $5,000 is a red flag on duct sizing.

### If it has radiators (steam or hot water) — no ducts

Very common in pre-1950 Montclair, Maplewood, South Orange, Summit, Cranford, Westfield and
Morristown. **There is no cheap answer here.**

| Approach | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Conventional ducted retrofit | $10,000 – $26,000+ | Often **not viable at any price** — no chases; you lose closets or ceiling height |
| **High-velocity** (Unico / SpacePak) | **$10,500 – $14,000** typical NJ | Purpose-built for this housing: 2–3" flexible tubing through existing wall cavities. Dehumidifies well. Fewer competent installers here, which is itself a price driver |
| Ductless mini-splits, 1 zone | $4,300 – $6,500 | |
| **Ductless, whole house (4–6 zones)** | **$14,000 – $25,000** | Wall units are visually intrusive — a real resale issue in period homes. Ceiling cassettes hide better, cost more |

Real quoted jobs (EnergySage 2025 marketplace, national): ductless averaged **$19,556**,
ducted **$14,529** — note ductless is *more* expensive, not less.

> **For a buyer:** a radiator house with no AC carries a **$10,000–$25,000 deferred cost.**
> That's not a rounding error against the purchase price — negotiate it, don't absorb it.
> The strategic upside: mini-splits also *heat*, so that spend buys a path off oil/gas.

**Rebates:** NJ Whole Home Energy Efficiency up to **$7,500** (plus 0% on-bill financing to
$25,000), and utility instant rebates ($60–$200 central AC; $300–$1,000 heat pumps, best tier
for cold-climate units). Requires a **BPI-certified participating contractor** — that
constrains who you can hire. There is **no federal credit in 2026.**

**Permits:** required statewide, and cheap ($50–$500). The value isn't the cost — it's that
**unpermitted HVAC work surfaces at resale and during insurance claims.** Ask a seller for
permits on any recent system work; recent equipment with no permit record is a negotiating point.

*Sources: [PSE&G/partner utility HVAC rebate form, eff. 1/1/2025](https://residential.energysavenj.com/jersey-central/sites/jersey-central/files/2025-03/PSEG%20HVAC%20Partner%20Utility%20Claim%20Form%20(Final)%20250311.pdf) · [NJ Clean Energy Program](https://cleanenergy.nj.gov/programs) · [IRS — 25C termination](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/faqs-for-modification-of-sections-25c-25d-25e-30c-30d-45l-45w-and-179d-under-public-law-119-21-139-stat-72-july-4-2025-commonly-known-as-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-obbb) · [EnergySage heat pump costs](https://www.energysage.com/heat-pumps/costs-and-benefits-air-source-heat-pumps/) · NJ contractor pricing (C&C Air, [lead-gen-derived by their own disclosure](https://candcair.com/blog/ac-replacement-cost/))*

## Modernizing a dated kitchen

**The headline finding: keeping the cabinet boxes is the only kitchen project that pays for
itself.** Everything past it halves the return, then halves it again.

All Cost vs. Value kitchen figures assume a **200 sq ft kitchen with 30 linear feet of
cabinetry** — a normal suburban NJ kitchen.

| Tier | NY metro cost | **Recouped** |
|---|---|---|
| Cosmetic refresh *(not a CvV category)* | **$12,000 – $30,000** | no survey data |
| **Minor remodel, midrange** — keep boxes, new fronts/counters/appliances | **$31,419** | **103.1%** |
| Major remodel, midrange — new cabinets, island, layout | $90,252 | 46.9% |
| Major remodel, upscale — custom cabinets, stone, commercial-grade | $183,172 | 33.8% |

**Cosmetic refresh, itemized (NJ):** cabinet painting/refinishing $1,500–$4,500 · cabinet
**refacing** $100–$350/linear ft ($4,500–$15,000 typical) · quartz or granite counters
$30–$150/sq ft installed · appliance package $8,000–$15,000 standard · backsplash $8–$25/sq ft.

**⚠️ Sources disagree ~2x on a gut kitchen.** Cost vs. Value says $90,252; NJ design-build
firms serving Essex/Union/Morris quote **$35,000–$85,000**. The gap is scope: CvV prices a
full general-contractor job including permits, electrical, flooring, painting, appliances and
GC overhead; contractor pages often quote cabinets + counters + install. **Practical framing:
budget $60,000–$90,000 for a real gut kitchen in these towns. Anything under $45,000 is
either a small kitchen or an incomplete scope.**

> **The takeaway:** the minor remodel (~$31k, keeps the boxes) is the **only interior project
> in the entire 2025 report that returns roughly 100%.** Spend past it because you want to
> live in it — not because it pays back.

*Sources: [Cost vs. Value 2025 — New York metro](https://www.jlconline.com/cost-vs-value/2025/new-york-ny/) · [Middle Atlantic](https://www.jlconline.com/cost-vs-value/2025/middle-atlantic/) · [full project specs PDF](https://prc-pa.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Cost-vs.-Value-Report-Philadelphia-Region-2025.pdf) · [Chapter Design-Build NJ kitchen guide](https://www.hellochapter.com/blog/new-jersey-kitchen-renovation-cost-guide/)*

## Bathrooms

| Project | NY metro cost | **Recouped** |
|---|---|---|
| Cosmetic refresh | **$3,500 – $9,000** | no survey data |
| **Full remodel, midrange (5'×7')** | **$30,376** | **76.5%** |
| Remodel, upscale (expand 35 → 100 sq ft) | $90,686 | 40.1% |
| Universal/accessible design | $47,052 | 64.7% |
| Powder room carved from existing space | **$6,500 – $15,000** | — |
| Bathroom *addition* (new 6'×8' over crawlspace) | $70,419 | 52.5% |

NJ practical bands for a full bath: budget from **$16,500**, mid-grade **$26,000**, high-end
**$37,500**.

**Biggest cost avoidance in a refresh: reglaze the tub ($500–$900) instead of replacing it.**
Replacing the tub is what drags in the tile surround, the valve, and often the subfloor.

**What actually sets the price of adding a bath — the drain, not the supply.** Water lines are
easy. The **waste line needs continuous ¼"-per-foot slope** back to the main stack, and there's
only so much depth in a joist bay. More than ~10–15 ft from the stack and it often can't be
drained conventionally.

| Situation | Plumbing cost |
|---|---|
| Ties into existing plumbing (back-to-back, or same wet wall) | **$500 – $2,000** |
| Full new rough-in (supply + drain + vent) | **$3,000 – $8,000** |
| Basement bath — below the sewer line, needs an ejector pump | add **$1,500 – $4,000** |

**⚠️ NJ-specific risk:** much of the Union/Essex/Morris stock is pre-1960 with **cast-iron
waste stacks.** Cutting into a 70-year-old stack sometimes reveals it needs replacing —
**$3,000–$8,000** that appears in no published range. **Budget a 15–20% contingency** on any
bathroom work in older housing.

> A bathroom addition recoups ~52% — worse than remodeling one. The case for adding a bath is
> that a 4-bed/1-bath is hard to sell, not that the money comes back.

## The NJ landmines — check these *before* you fall in love

### A damp basement — the one that hides behind a finished wall

Added 2026-07-21, because a real appraisal hit it and this document had nothing to say. If a
basement smells musty, or there is a **dedicated dehumidifier running**, that is not a quirk —
it is active water management, and the seller knows.

**The finish is the problem, not the smell.** An unfinished damp basement is a known quantity
you can look at. A *finished* one means the question is what is behind the drywall, and
framing and insulation against a damp block wall is exactly where concealed mould grows. Treat
a finished basement in a house with a dehumidifier as a room you cannot inspect.

| Item | Typical NJ | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Whole job, most houses** | **$5,000 – $12,000** | full perimeter drainage, good pump with battery backup, wall treatment |
| Range across all severities | $3,000 – $15,000 | |
| Basic interior system | $2,500 – $5,000 | interior drainage, basic pump, vapour barrier |
| **Interior french drain** | **$6,000 – $10,000** | under-slab, the usual answer for block foundations |
| French drain, interior *or* exterior | $5,000 – $15,000 | exterior means excavating the foundation |
| Sump pump, standard | $800 – $1,500 | installed |
| Sump pump, dual + battery backup | $1,800 – $3,000 | the backup is the point — pumps fail in the storm |
| **Mould remediation** | **$1,000 – $4,000** | severity-dependent |
| Mould, by area | $50 – $75 / sq ft | how the big numbers happen |
| Foundation crack repair | $75 – $150 / linear ft | |

**Three things worth knowing before you talk to anyone.**

1. **An interior system does not stop water — it manages it.** It collects what comes through
   and pumps it out. Sellers describing a basement as "waterproofed" usually mean this.
2. **Tearing out finished walls to inspect or remediate is its own cost**, and it is not in the
   numbers above. A finished basement can easily double the job.
3. **Damp basement and radon travel together** — the same block-wall and slab pathways carry
   both. If one is present, test for the other; mitigation is $3,000–$5,000 on a complex block
   foundation (see Radon, below).

> **For a buyer:** a musty finished basement is a **$5,000–$12,000 exposure with a fat tail**,
> and unlike a roof you cannot see what you are buying. Get a moisture reading and a mould
> inspection inside the contingency period — not a look and a sniff.

### Buried oil tank — the deal-killer

If the house ever had oil heat, this is the item that can end a purchase. **Order a tank sweep
($150–$500) before you're emotionally committed.**

| Situation | Cost |
|---|---|
| **Tank sweep / locate** (ground radar + magnetometer) | **$150 – $500** |
| Clean removal, no leak (incl. soil sampling) | **$1,600 – $3,000** |
| Abandonment in place (filled, per NJ code) | $1,000 – $2,500 |
| Low-level contamination | $3,000 – $6,000 |
| **Most common leak cleanup** | **$8,000 – $10,000** |
| Complex remediation | $12,000 – $25,000+ |
| **Bad leak, full tail** | **$4,000 → $100,000+** |

**The one trigger that changes everything:** NJDEP requires **groundwater sampling** when any
part of the tank sits within the seasonal high water table or **within 2 feet of groundwater or
bedrock.** That is the difference between a $9,000 job and a $60,000+ one.

**Worst case, concretely:** a 1950s colonial with a 550-gallon tank 6 ft down near groundwater,
weeping slowly for 20 years, plume running under the footing. Delineation borings → excavation
requiring **foundation underpinning** → regulated-waste disposal → **groundwater monitoring
wells sampled over multiple quarters** → LSRP oversight. **$75,000–$150,000+ over 18–36 months.**
No 45-day closing survives it.

**Five things most buyers get wrong:**
1. **NJDEP does not require removing a non-leaking tank.** Lenders, insurers and buyers do.
2. Only a **closure-certified UST firm** may legally remove one — a general contractor cannot.
3. **The state reimbursement fund is effectively dead.** NJDEP: new applications "shall not be
   reviewed or processed at this time due to insufficient funds," with a ~3.5-year wait. **Don't
   let a seller argue the state will cover it.** File anyway (free, preserves position), but
   underwrite the whole cost yourself.
4. **A clean removal produces a closure report + municipal sign-off, not an NFA letter.** Buyers
   and agents routinely demand an NFA that cannot exist. Ask for the **closure report with
   passing soil analyticals.**
5. **Abandonment-in-place is the trap.** It's legal and permanent — but it certifies nothing
   about the soil, and **the liability travels with the land to you.**

**Real leverage:** NJDEP requires the contractor to give you a written contract with **a maximum
price that cannot be exceeded without a written amendment.** Insist on it — it converts
open-ended tail risk into a capped number. Tanks are also an enumerated item on the NJ Seller's
Property Condition Disclosure.

*Sources: 🟢 [NJDEP Unregulated Heating Oil Tank FAQs](https://dep.nj.gov/srp/unregulated/unregulated-faqs/) · [NJDEP UST program](https://dep.nj.gov/srp/unregulated/) · [PUST fund fact sheet](https://dep.nj.gov/grantandloanprograms/fact-sheets/unregulated-heating-oil-underground-storage-tank/) · 🔵 [Curren Environmental NJ](https://www.currenenvironmental.com/how-much-does-an-oil-tank-removal-cost-in-new-jersey) · [ERC/oiltankremovalnj](https://oiltankremovalnj.us/how-much-does-soil-remediation-cost/)*

### Radon

| | Cost |
|---|---|
| Professional test | $100 – $150 |
| **Mitigation, typical single-family** | **$1,000 – $1,800** |
| Complex (multi-wing, crawl space, block foundation) | $3,000 – $5,000 |
| Well-water radon treatment (separate job) | $4,000 – $6,000 |

Action level is **4 pCi/L**. Roughly **60,000 radon tests happen in NJ each year and 75–80% are
real-estate driven** — testing is standard practice here, not optional diligence.

**NJ has a real disclosure law with a specific shape:** a seller must give you **existing test
results and evidence of any mitigation.** It's a duty to hand over what they have — *not* a duty
to test. A seller who never tested has nothing to disclose; a seller who tested and got a bad
number **cannot bury it.**

Higher-radon geology (the **Reading Prong**) runs under northern Hunterdon, western Morris,
Sussex and Warren. **Tier is assigned by municipality, not county** — look up the specific town
rather than assuming. NJ requires **NJDEP certification** for radon contractors; national
certification alone isn't sufficient.

*Sources: 🟢 [NJDEP Radon](https://dep.nj.gov/rpp/radon/) · [Radon & Real Estate](https://dep.nj.gov/rpp/radon/radon-real-estate/) · [Radon DataMiner — results by town](https://njems.nj.gov/DataMiner/Search/SearchByCategory?isExternal=y&getCategory=y&catName=Radon) · [EPA radon reduction guide](https://www.epa.gov/radon/consumers-guide-radon-reduction-how-fix-your-home)*

### Asbestos

Pre-1980 NJ basements very commonly have asbestos pipe wrap on steam/hot-water mains and tape
at duct joints.

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| **Basement pipe insulation (typical NJ job)** | **$1,850 – $3,000** |
| Larger pipe scope | $2,800 – $11,000 |
| Boiler/furnace jacket | $1,500 – $4,000 |
| 9×9 floor tile + mastic | $5 – $15/sq ft |
| Whole home | $5,700 – $20,000+ |

**The rule that matters: NJ does not require removing intact, undisturbed asbestos.**
Encapsulation is legal and far cheaper. **But any boiler or HVAC job that disturbs it converts a
legal non-issue into mandatory abatement** — which must happen *before* the mechanical work, so
you get schedule risk on top of cost. **This is the most commonly omitted line in NJ boiler
quotes.** Looking at a 1920s house with wrapped steam mains? Assume **$2,000–$3,000 sits on top
of any boiler number below.**

### Knob-and-tube wiring and a 100-amp panel

| | Cost |
|---|---|
| **Panel upgrade, 100A → 200A** | **$2,500 – $4,500** |
| Add if the utility drop/meter also needs work | +$500 – $1,500 |

**Most pre-1970 NJ housing is still on 100 amps.** This upgrade gets quietly triggered by almost
everything desirable: central AC, a heat pump, a dormer or addition, a standby generator, an EV
charger. Budget it as a near-certainty on an older house you plan to modernize.

## Heating and hot water

| Item | Typical | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Gas furnace | **$5,000 – $7,000** | $3,500 – $12,000 |
| **Steam boiler** | **$8,000 – $12,000** | $6,000 – $16,000 |
| Hot-water boiler | $8,000 – $13,000 | $6,000 – $18,000 |
| Steam → hot-water conversion | **$20,000 – $25,000** | $15,000 – $30,000+ |
| Oil → gas conversion | add $2,000 – $4,500 | *plus tank removal* |
| Water heater, tank | **$1,500 – $2,400** | $1,200 – $4,100 |
| Water heater, tankless | $3,850 – $6,500 | $2,800 – $9,000 |

**Three things worth knowing before you talk to anyone:**

1. **A "95% efficient steam boiler" does not exist.** Steam needs ~180°F return temperatures, so
   condensing operation is thermodynamically unavailable — max practical efficiency is ~82–86%.
   Anyone quoting one is selling something imaginary. It also means steam boilers are often
   **ineligible** for the AFUE≥90 utility rebate that a hot-water house would get.
2. **On a steam job, the low bid is a warning sign.** The money and the competence go into the
   near-boiler piping — header, risers, equalizer. Built off-spec, the system carries water over
   and never works right. This is the near-unanimous view among steam specialists.
3. **The orphaned water heater.** Pulling an old 80%-efficiency furnace off a shared masonry
   chimney can leave the water heater alone on an oversized flue, which then needs **relining
   ($1,000–$2,500)** or conversion to power-vent. It's the single most commonly missed line item
   in furnace quotes.

**Rebate trap:** the $540–$1,000 furnace/boiler rebates require replacing a **non-condensing**
unit. A house with a 20-year-old 80% boiler qualifies; one with a 12-year-old 92% unit doesn't.

## New roof

**First, measure the right thing.** Almost every cost page says "2,000 sq ft home" but its
table is really per 2,000 sq ft *of roof* — and those aren't the same number. A 2,000 sq ft
two-story colonial has an 1,100–1,400 sq ft footprint, which at typical 6/12–8/12 pitch is
about **18–22 squares** (a "square" is 100 sq ft). A cape is 15–18. **Price off measured roof
squares, never house size.**

### Asphalt / architectural shingle — 20-square colonial, one layer torn off

| | Cost |
|---|---|
| Low | $9,000 – $11,000 |
| **Most common** | **$13,000 – $18,000** |
| High (steep, cut-up, 2 layers, some decking) | $20,000 – $26,000 |

Per square installed: 3-tab $350–$650 · **architectural $450–$800** · designer $650–$1,400.
County all-in figures from one NJ roofer for a 2,000 sq ft home: **Essex $12,000–$18,000,
Morris $12,000–$18,000, Passaic $11,000–$17,000, Bergen $13,000–$20,000.** NJ roofing labor
runs $55–$95/hr against $45–$70 nationally; municipal permits $200–$500.

**⚠️ The one big source disagreement — and it's a 2–3× gap.** NJ contractor pages quote
$4.50–$8.00/sq ft. Cost vs. Value implies **~$12.40/sq ft** ($37,335 for 30 squares). That's
not noise: CvV prices a full-service remodeler's job with real overhead and profit, skylight
flashing and disposal; contractor landing pages quote the lean end to win the phone call.
**Treat contractor pricing as the floor and CvV as the ceiling, and budget the middle.**

### Tear-off, and the layer rule that can cost you $3,000

- One layer: $1.00–$1.50/sq ft (~$2,000–$3,000 on a 20-square roof), usually already in the quote
- **Two layers: $1.50–$2.50/sq ft — a $1,000–$3,000 surcharge**

> **NJ code allows a maximum of two asphalt layers.** If a house already has two, a re-cover is
> illegal and **full tear-off is mandatory** — not optional, not negotiable. This is common in
> older Union and Essex stock where a 1980s–90s roof went straight over the original. **Ask the
> seller or inspector how many layers are up there.** It's a $1,000–$3,000 swing that is
> perfectly knowable before you close.

### Rotted decking — the classic mid-job change order

Invisible until the old roof comes off. **$50–$125 per 4×8 sheet installed**; most houses need
2–8 sheets (**$150–$1,000**). Partial replacement $500–$2,500; full $3,000–$8,000+. Overall it
raises a roof job **10–25%**.

> **Get the per-sheet rate written into the contract before work starts.** Once the roof is
> open, you have zero leverage on that price.

### Premium materials (same 20-square roof)

| Material | Per sq ft | Total |
|---|---|---|
| Metal shingles | $7 – $12 | $14,000 – $24,000 |
| Cedar shingles | $9 – $15 | $18,000 – $30,000 |
| **Standing-seam metal** | $9 – $17 | **$18,000 – $35,000** |
| Cedar shake | $10 – $19 | $20,000 – $38,000 |
| Clay / concrete tile | $8 – $25 | $16,000 – $50,000 |
| **Natural slate** | **$15 – $40** | **$30,000 – $75,000+** |

**Slate deserves its own paragraph**, because it's on plenty of older Montclair, Summit and
Morristown houses and it's the number that can reprice an offer. Full replacement is
$30,000–$75,000+. **But slate lasts 75–150 years, so it is usually *repairable* rather than
replaceable — and it's normally the flashing, not the slate, that fails first.** Slate repair is
priced as skilled time-and-materials, not per square.

> ⚠️ **Honest gap:** I could not source a reliable NJ figure for slate *repair* — the specialist
> pricing pages blocked access. For a slate-roofed house, repair-vs-replace is the decision that
> makes or breaks the deal, and it's worth a direct call to one of the handful of slate
> specialists serving Essex/Morris rather than any web estimate.

### What drives the range

**Pitch** is the biggest labor multiplier — above ~7/12 crews need staging and fall protection,
**+15–25% on labor**, and many NJ colonials are 8/12 or steeper. Then: **layers** (see above),
**decking condition** (unknowable until tear-off — the real reason quotes come as ranges),
**penetrations** (chimneys $200–$500 each, skylights $300–$600, pipe boots $50–$150, chimney
cricket $300–$600), **access** (tight lots with nowhere to put a dumpster — common in Union
County and older Essex towns), **stories**, and **season** (winter runs 5–15% cheaper; summer is
peak). Materials are up ~3.1% year-over-year and ~45% above 2020.

### Does it pay back? Less than you'd think

| | Job cost | **Recouped** |
|---|---|---|
| **Asphalt shingles — NY metro** | $37,335 | **57.0%** |
| Asphalt — Middle Atlantic | $37,201 | 58.3% |
| Asphalt — national | $31,871 | 67.5% |
| **Metal roofing — NY metro** | $60,246 | **44.8%** |

**⚠️ Correcting a claim you'll see repeated:** several NJ and NY contractor blogs say asphalt
roofing recoups "about 68% in the Middle Atlantic." **That's the national number.** The actual
Middle Atlantic figure is **58.3%**, and New York metro is **57.0%** — roughly 10 points worse.
Roofing is one of the *weakest*-recouping exterior projects in this region; siding and door
replacement far outperform it. A new roof is something you buy to stop water and close a deal,
not an investment.

## Siding, windows and the rest of the exterior

### Siding

| Material | Installed | Whole house (~2,200 sq ft of siding) |
|---|---|---|
| Vinyl | $7 – $12/sq ft | **$15,000 – $26,000** |
| Insulated vinyl | $10 – $16/sq ft | $18,000 – $42,000 |
| **Fiber cement (James Hardie)** | $11 – $20/sq ft | **$24,000 – $44,000** |
| Cedar shake | $13 – $22/sq ft | $29,000 – $48,000 |

Recouped (Middle Atlantic): fiber cement **~99%**, vinyl **~96%** — though reports of the same
survey range from 77% to 97%, so don't treat any single siding ROI number as settled. Both
comfortably beat windows.

**Two NJ add-ons that aren't in the headline price:** hidden **sheathing rot** ($300–$1,500 per
area — carry a 10–15% contingency on older homes), and **asbestos-cement siding**, which is
common in pre-1980 stock. Budget **$3–$8/sq ft** for abatement (**+$6,000–$18,000** on a typical
house) and have it quoted **separately by a licensed abatement firm**, never bundled into a
siding bid. Three-story work roughly **doubles** per-square-foot labor.

### Windows — and the historic-district trap

| Type | Per window | Full colonial (~22 windows) |
|---|---|---|
| Vinyl insert | $800 – $1,400 | **$18,000 – $31,000** |
| Fiberglass / composite | $1,200 – $2,400 | $26,000 – $53,000 |
| Wood or wood-clad | $1,400 – $2,600 | $31,000 – $57,000 |
| **Historic-compliant (wood, full-frame, simulated divided light)** | **$2,000 – $3,500+** | **$45,000 – $77,000+** |

**Windows are the weakest exterior ROI** — 75.5% vinyl / 70.1% wood nationally, and reportedly
only ~60–70% in the Middle Atlantic.

**⚠️ Montclair, Maplewood, South Orange, Morristown and Madison have Historic Preservation
Commissions** that require a Certificate of Appropriateness for exterior changes visible from
the street — **windows explicitly included.** Vinyl is generally not permitted on street-facing
elevations. The compliant spec (wood, full-frame, SDL with spacer bars) runs **2–2.5× a vinyl
insert job — roughly +$25,000 to $45,000 on a colonial.**

> **The cheaper path the commissions actually prefer: restore the original sash and add storm
> windows — $300–$550 per window**, about a fifth the cost of compliant replacement. Given how
> weakly window replacement recoups, this is often the better *financial* call too, not just the
> better preservation one.

Also: the **federal window credit expired 12/31/2025** (nothing in 2026), but NJ's **Historic
Property Reinvestment Act** offers a **25% credit capped at $25,000** for board-approved
rehabilitation of a qualified historic property.

### Gutters, chimney, exterior paint

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Aluminum gutters, whole house (150–200 lf) | **$1,800 – $3,000** |
| Copper gutters | $3,000 – $8,000 |
| Fascia repair (the classic hidden cost) | $6 – $20/lf → **+$1,000 – $3,500** |
| Gutter guards | $3 – $28/lf ⚠️ *10× spread — roofer-installed mesh vs. branded direct-sales* |
| Underground downspout drainage | $3,200 – $8,500 |
| **Chimney Level 2 inspection** (standard at sale) | **$200 – $500** |
| Chimney cap / flashing / crown | $150–$450 · $300–$800 · $600–$1,200 |
| Repointing | $500 – $2,500 |
| **Stainless liner** | **$1,200 – $4,000** (~$2,500 typical) |
| Partial rebuild above roofline | $2,000 – $6,000 |
| Full rebuild | $4,000 – $15,000 ⚠️ *sources describe different scopes — pin it down in writing* |
| **Exterior repaint, 2,000–2,500 sq ft colonial** | **$6,500 – $10,000** vinyl/aluminum · **$8,000 – $14,000** wood with real prep |

**Get a Level 2 chimney inspection at purchase** — NFPA 211 specifies one on sale or transfer,
and it's the one that puts a camera down the flue. The signature North Jersey finding is a
**flue sized for an old coal or oil boiler now venting a modern gas unit**: the cooler, wetter
exhaust condenses and destroys the masonry from the inside. That's why **an oil-to-gas
conversion requires relining.**

**⚠️ Lead paint is the dominant exterior-paint variable.** The EPA RRP rule covers any paid work
disturbing **more than 20 sq ft** of exterior painted surface on a **pre-1978** home — which is
essentially every repaint of an older NJ house, since 20 sq ft of scraping happens in the first
hour. It requires a certified firm, a certified renovator on site, and 10-foot ground
containment. **Premium: +$1,500–$4,000.** Penalties run to $49,772 per violation per day, so
legitimate contractors will not skip it.

**A few of these deserve a sentence:**

- **Composite decking beats wood on ROI here** (97.8% vs 94.0% in the Middle Atlantic), which
  *inverts* the national pattern — regional buyers appear to price in freeze-thaw durability.
- **Finishing a basement recoups worse in NJ than nationally** (58% vs 71%), partly because NJ
  appraisal convention excludes below-grade area from headline square footage. Also: **finishing
  an existing basement does not require an egress window unless you create a bedroom** (2021 IRC
  R310.7) — most contractor websites get this wrong. If you do need one, it's $6,000–$10,000,
  and **pre-1940 fieldstone foundations are the expensive case** ($7,500–$9,500+) because the
  wall needs an engineer before it's cut.
- **Additions are the worst return in the entire report** (28% midrange, 17% upscale). And the
  biggest costs never appear on the quote: **moving out for 4–8 months ($20,000–$50,000)**, the
  new staircase eating 80–120 sq ft of your existing first floor, and design fees at 10–17%.
  **Assume you need a zoning variance** on an older non-conforming lot — even to build straight
  up over your own footprint — which adds 3–4 months and an escrow that's billed against, not capped.
- **Driveways: replace in kind, don't expand.** Impervious-coverage caps are the binding
  constraint, and real ordinances in our towns are tight — **Chatham Twp 40%** of a lot under
  10,000 sq ft, **East Brunswick 25–40%** by lot size, **Cranford R-3 38%**. A typical older lot
  with a house, walk and patio has almost no headroom, so **widening a driveway even a few feet
  can force a bulk variance** — zoning board, neighbor notice, engineer, months. Replacing the
  same footprint sidesteps all of it. Two traps: **Hillsborough counts all driveways as 100%
  impervious regardless of material**, so gravel or "permeable" pavers buy no relief there; and
  while a driveway is a *zoning* matter rather than a building-code one, **redoing the apron at
  the curb needs a separate right-of-way permit** (county or NJDOT if it's not a municipal road).
  Also note a **flood hazard area triggers an NJDEP individual permit regardless of size** —
  that's a location trigger, not a square-footage one.
- **Asphalt quotes are moving right now.** NJDOT publishes a real monthly asphalt price index:
  binder went **$530/ton in January 2026 → $715 in mid-July 2026, about +35%**, with diesel up
  ~38% over the same stretch. **A quote from late 2025 or early 2026 is stale and will be
  re-priced.** ([NJDOT price index](https://dot.nj.gov/transportation/business/aashtoware/PriceIndex.shtm))
  And be careful comparing bids: NJ contractors quote anywhere from $4 to $15/sq ft for "a
  driveway," and the gap is almost entirely **unstated spec** — 2" of asphalt on the old base
  versus 3" over 8" of new compacted base, which is what freeze-thaw here actually requires.
- **Solar economics changed for 2026.** The 30% federal credit is gone — a ~$6,900 swing on a
  $23,000 system. What remains is NJ's **SREC-II at roughly $76.50–$85/MWh for 15 years**
  (~$800–$1,200/yr), plus sales-tax and property-tax exemptions. Payback is now 7–10 years,
  about 2–3 years longer than for a 2025 buyer. **A house with existing *owned* solar is a
  genuine asset** — the prior owner ate the capital cost while the credit still existed, and the
  SREC payments transfer. *Leased* solar usually adds nothing and complicates the sale.

### If you're looking at a house with an in-ground pool

| | Cost |
|---|---|
| Partial removal ("punch and fill") | **$6,000 – $12,000** |
| **Full removal** | **$12,000 – $25,000** |

**Pools recoup 56% — the lowest of any outdoor project NAR measures**, and that's the *national*
figure; in a 3–4 month season it's often flat to negative. A pool also triggers an NJ **added
assessment**, taxed on market value added.

**The $6,000–$15,000 difference between partial and full removal buys three real things:**
1. **Buildability.** After a partial fill you can *never* build over that footprint — no
   addition, garage, or patio slab. On a tight lot that permanently sterilizes 500+ sq ft.
2. **No long-term settling** — under-perforated shells dish out over years in clay soils.
3. **No disclosure problem.** NJ sellers must disclose latent material defects and **an "as-is"
   clause does not waive that duty.** A buried shell that makes yard area unbuildable is a
   textbook latent defect — so the money "saved" reappears as a negotiation against you at resale.

**Access drives the price more than pool size.** Older Union/Essex towns platted on 40–50 ft lots
have 6–10 ft side yards; an excavator needs 6–7 ft. If fence sections must come down, add 15–25%;
if nothing larger than a walk-behind fits, expect the top of the range or worse.
